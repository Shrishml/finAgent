**Status:** Draft v1

Last week I sat down with my father and calculated the actual return on his LIC policy.

The agent had sold it as "3x maturity in 20 years." Sounds great. Triple your money.

The actual CAGR? ~6.5%.

His personal loan interest rate? 12%.

He's been earning 6.5% on his "investment" while paying 12% on his loan. For years. Nobody pointed this out — not the agent, not the app, not the policy document.

This got me curious. I started asking around. Turns out this pattern is everywhere:

— A friend has 7 mutual funds across 3 apps. He thinks he's diversified. When we actually compared the holdings, 5 of them hold the same top 15 stocks. He's managing 7 funds for the diversification of 2.

— Another friend has 15 mutual funds. Bank RM recommended some, he started others after reading blogs. Now he wants to consolidate but can't figure out the tax implications. So he does nothing.

— A colleague's parents were told their FD is "safe." It is. It's also earning 7% pre-tax — about 5% after tax in the 30% bracket. They're barely beating inflation and calling it savings.

Every single one of these has a correct, computable answer. XIRR, CAGR, overlap analysis, tax-optimized exit — it's just math.

And honestly? I'm no different. I have MFs, PF, NPS, some stocks — spread across apps and portals. If you asked me "what's your actual CAGR across your entire portfolio?" I couldn't tell you. Nobody can. There's no single place that connects all of it.

But the people selling these products have zero incentive to do this math for you. And the apps that could? They show you returns in green and hope you don't ask questions.

I've been talking to ~15 people about their finances over the past two weeks. The same sentence keeps coming up:

"I know something is off, but I don't know what to calculate."

Does this resonate? What's the one number about your own finances you suspect is wrong but haven't verified?
