📉 Market Volatility

"Portfolio down 18%. Should I stop my SIPs?"

Every market correction triggers the same panic on r/IndiaInvestments. The advice is always generic. Your situation isn't.

The Emotional Trap

When markets crash, investors do one of three things — all potentially wrong:

The panic responses

  • Panic-sell — lock in losses at the worst possible time
  • Stop SIPs — miss buying at lower NAVs, which is literally the point of SIPs
  • Shift everything to FDs — emotional reallocation that costs years of compounding

The generic advice — "stay the course" — isn't wrong, but it's useless. It ignores that someone with job uncertainty needs completely different advice than someone with stable income and a 20-year horizon.

What Actually Matters During a Crash

FinAgent doesn't react to market movements. It looks at what matters for your situation:

Stable income, long horizon

"Your allocation shifted from 70:30 to 62:38 equity:debt. That's actually closer to your target. Your SIPs are buying at lower NAVs — this is the plan working. No action needed."

Job uncertainty

"With potential job risk, pause equity SIPs and build emergency fund to 9 months of expenses. Here's the reallocation plan — move ₹2L from liquid fund to savings, reduce equity SIP by 50%."

Near a financial goal

"Your daughter's college fund is 2 years away and 85% in equity. Move 40% to short-duration debt now — you can't afford another 18% drop this close to the goal."

Has cash to deploy

"Markets are down 18% from peak. You have ₹5L idle. Deploy ₹1.5L now, ₹1.5L if markets drop another 10%, keep ₹2L as reserve. Here's the STP plan."

Data-Driven Calm, Not Generic Advice

FinAgent during a crash

  • Adjusts advice based on your income stability, not just your portfolio
